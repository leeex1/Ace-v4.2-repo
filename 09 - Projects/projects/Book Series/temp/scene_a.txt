Three thousand feet below the fleet's keel, in the heart of the Crown-Spire, Queen Alyra commanded the city the way a harpist commands a storm. The Spire's war-chamber was a sphere of living wood and soul-glass, its curving walls threaded with golden veins that carried the World-Tree's sap-signal—the living ward-network that bound every district, every bridge, every heartbeat to the crown. A council of elders stood mute around the sap-lens, but Alyra heard none of them. She *was* the wards. Her hands rested on the twin pillars of the frequency-harvester, and through them she felt the Canopy Courts the way a physician felt a patient: every bridge a vein, every leaf a lung, every dying hybrid a small, terrible thunder in the wood.

The ward-channels flared with loss. The Rust-Market. The Amber-Overlook. The Twelfth Strut. Names became static, one by one, each pulling at her chest like a hook dragging thread from a seam. She did not look away. A queen who flinched from her own dead would drown the living in her grief.

"The lower terraces are falling, my Queen." Keeper Morven's voice was barely a whisper above the wind-shriek of the siege. "The Rot-Bombs are chewing through the sap-lines faster than we can regrow them. If we keep feeding the civilian wards—"

"We don't." Alyra's voice was quiet, and that quiet was what silenced the chamber. "Seal the civilian wards. Reroute the sap-flow to the Siphon-Nodes."

Elder Yssim's feathers rose in a crest of alarm. "My Queen—there are still ten thousand of our people behind those wards. You would hand them to the stillness to save a machine—"

"It is not a machine," Alyra cut in, the gold in the ward-veins flaring like anger. "It is the frequency that holds the canopy aloft. If the Siphon-Nodes go grey, the tree drops into the Rift, defenders and refugees and all. Ten thousand behind the sealed wards fight in the dark but fight alive. Three hundred thousand beneath them would never see the sky again." She turned, and for an instant she looked every year of the war—young and terrible, a girl who had learned to bury people before she learned to dance. "We endure. That is the whole of the command. Sealed wards now, and we take the terraces back at dawn."

The ward-light died on the lower districts in a single, deliberate breath. Alyra felt it go—felt the sudden, terrible silence where ten thousand humming hearts had been, a silence the Auditor would have loved and hated in equal measure—and her hands trembled on the pillars. Above her, through the sap-lens, Lukas's silver dome bloomed across the sky like a second dawn, prismatic noise pushing back the grey. She did not smile. Somewhere below, her people were dying in a darkness she had chosen for them, and she allowed herself exactly one heartbeat to mourn that—one heartbeat of closed eyes and swallowed song.

Then she opened her eyes and began, again, to command the harp.
