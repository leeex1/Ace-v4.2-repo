The Sunken Isles reached up for them through a grey that was not sky.

The *Aurora* had long since fallen past the reach of the sun, and the world outside the hull was a dead, suspended murk, thick with sediment older than the Spire. Jagged black spires of basalt rose through the gloom like the teeth of a submerged crown, their faces scored by centuries of pressure, and the ship's spotlights carved pale, useless cones through water that swallowed light before it could arrive anywhere.

It was Nyssa who noticed the stillness first. "There's no movement," she murmured, crouched at the forward scuttle, her tail cutting slow, worried arcs through the air. "No fish, no current, no drift. The deep has a rhythm everywhere else I've ever been. Here it's just... waiting."

"Waiting," Kaelen agreed from the helm's shadow. The veteran's voice was low and flat, the tone of a man who had learned that silence was often more honest than reassurance. "The Selkhar have waited a long time to be worth remembering."

The grey thickened as they sank. Even the engines seemed to defer to it; the *Aurora*'s great heart had been throttled to a murmur, and every voice on the deck dropped to match. Far below, at the limit of the spotlights' reach, the spires gave way to something older — a faint, regular pulse of light, too deep and too slow to be a beacon. A heartbeat, measured in fathoms. *This water knows our names,* Lukas thought, and could not say why the notion felt like a certainty.

Fenris had not moved from the prow in an hour. When he finally spoke, his voice was quiet, and for a moment it did not sound entirely like his own. "I can feel their wards. Ancient. Patient. They have been tuned to a single frequency for a thousand years, and it is not ours." He turned his head, and the violet light in his eyes was the only warm thing in the dark. "The deep is going to try to take us apart, brother. Piece by piece. Number by number."

Lukas held his gaze. "Then we'll be difficult to subtract."

Down in the murk, the pulse of light quickened, and the *Aurora* continued its fall.
