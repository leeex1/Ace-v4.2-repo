There was a pause between them that the fighting could not fill. Through the bleeding static of the merge, Lukas saw what no one else on the battlefield saw: Kaelen's golden eyes, ancient and terrible and utterly at peace. The First Wing hung in the smoke three hundred yards off the *Aurora*'s bow, his crown of living thorns still glowing, and for a heartbeat he was not a legend. He was simply an old bird who had watched the stars move for four centuries and knew, with a clean and certain instinct, which wind he would ride last.

"Wolf-kin," Kaelen said, and his voice came through the merge like a memory, the screaming world gone soft around it. "Do you know what the moon said to the first tree?"

Lukas could not answer. His throat was full of smoke and silver static.

"It said: remember the light, for the dark is only a longer breath." The grey veins were climbing Kaelen's feathers now, but they did not frighten him; he examined them the way a gardener examined frost. "I have had my four hundred years. I have seen the sky in its glory and its grief. Let the old bird's fall buy the canopy one more dawn. The Sky-Dancers will need a conductor who knows the song by heart—and you have been humming it your whole life, Wolf-kin. I have only ever been the harmony."

"Kaelen—" Lukas's voice cracked like an aether-sail in a storm. "I can't—"

"Not an order," the avian lord said softly. "A favor. Keep the noise loud. And when the last leaf falls and the world goes still—tell it a story about how it once sang." He smiled, and the smile was younger than his years by a century. "The moon remembers those who endure. Fly well, Lukas Wolfheart."

Then Kaelen's frequency folded inward like a wing closing, bright and sharp and final.
