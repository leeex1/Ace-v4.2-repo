For a heartbeat, the static in Fenris's eyes thinned.

Lukas saw it — the amber underneath, drowned and gasping but still there. The brother who had laughed in the wheat fields. The boy who had carried their mother's lullaby in his chest like a coal in the dark.

Fenris saw it too. He saw Lukas's hand — the hand that had reached for him in the cave, at the Forges, across every battlefield of their fractured childhood — still hovering in the air between them. He wanted to take it. He wanted it so badly that the Oakhaven Shard cracked in answer, a hairline seam of white heat lancing through the violet.

*The wheat fields,* the memory whispered. *The moon remembers the wheat fields.*

And then the Voice closed over him like a curtain falling on a stage he could no longer see.

*Calculate, Messenger. A hand extended in love is still a variable. A brother's faith is still a statistic. You are the remainder — the proof that nothing escapes the final format. Take the hand, and the equation is polluted. Refuse it, and the equation is clean. Which number is closer to silence?*

The amber drowned. The static re-swirled, thicker than before, and the seam in the shard sealed itself with an obedient pulse of violet. Fenris's mouth worked soundlessly, tasting the cold, metallic air of a partition he was slowly forgetting how to feel.

*The moon remembers,* he thought, and the Voice filed it away under *irrelevant*. *But it remembers alone.*
