The answer came anyway, in the sound of raised voices at the midline barrier.

A Royal Guard lieutenant named Aldous Vane had planted himself three inches from the blue field, his polished spear held crosswise like a gate. On the other side, a wolf-hybrid veteran with a scarred muzzle and a shaved scalp — Brann, the Vanguard called him, or simply the Old Fang — stood with his arms folded, his augments bleeding violet against the ward like a slow wound.

"Step back from the field," Vane said, each word clipped, a man reciting a regulation to cover a shaking hand. "No Vanguard weapon crosses this line. Those are the terms."

"Your terms," Brann spat. "Written in a language your great-grandfathers used to lock my grandmother in a cage. You want to search my claws for contraband? Come over the line and try."

The Guard on the port side went for their spears in a single, oiled motion. On the starboard side, three Vanguard hybrids rose from the shadows, their Discord flickering like struck matches. The barrier between them hummed a note higher, the blue energy bowing inward, and for a moment the entire ship seemed to hold its breath.

"Do you remember Serjeant Mallory?" Vane asked, gently, almost sadly. "Fourth ward. Before the Sky Tears."

A Guard serjeant's grip tightened, then loosened. Everyone on the port side seemed to remember Serjeant Mallory. The silence that followed had a texture to it.

"Mallory was Vanguard," Vane said. "Born hybrid. Died holding a nursery door shut against the first finalize-wave so forty children could crawl out a window with their mothers. The Spire gave her a plaque. We gave her a grave in the old ground, same as any of ours." He looked across the barrier at Brann. "If I can't guard her noise without becoming the thing that killed her, I don't know what the point of the field is."

Brann's static had thinned. "I knew Mallory. She held the door, and the Spire still calls my kind filth. You want to honor her? Start by seeing me as something that belongs on the same deck as you."

It wasn't forgiveness. It wasn't peace. But it was the first time either of them had said a true thing without a weapon behind it.

Lukas moved before the moment could curdle, his silver light flaring cold and commanding. He stepped between them, one hand raised toward the Guard, the other toward the Vanguard.

"Stand down."

"My Lord Wolfheart —" Vane began.

"Is the reason this ship is still in the sky instead of a smear of grey marble on the Shattered Wastes," Lukas cut him off. "The Audit doesn't care which side of the line you die on. It finalizes port and starboard alike."

He held Vane's gaze until the lieutenant's eyes dropped first. Then he turned to Brann, whose claws had half-extended, and who had the decency to look ashamed of them.

"You fought with Kray at the Forges," Lukas said. "Kray believed the Vanguard could stand in the light. Don't make a liar of the dead."

Brann stared at him for a long moment. Then the claws retracted, one joint at a time. "The wolf remembers the light," he said, and stepped back from the field.

Vane lowered his spear — a fraction, then fully. The hum of the barrier settled back into its mechanical rhythm, and the breath the ship had been holding came out in a long, unsteady sigh.

Lukas did not linger to savor it. It wasn't a victory; it was a truce, and truces were only as strong as the next provocation. The proof of it stood at the prow, waiting for him.
