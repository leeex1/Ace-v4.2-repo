There was a moment, at the threshold, when the sea decided.

It came not as a wave but as a weight — the first true pressure of the Selkhar depths sliding over the hull like a hand closing on a throat. The *Aurora*'s iron bones sang a note no skyship had ever been tuned to play, a low groan that traveled up through the deck plating and into the teeth of every soul aboard. Ears popped. Teeth ached. The viewports went from grey mist to absolute black in the space of a held breath, and then the deep began to wake around them — pale green motes drifting past the glass like the eyes of things that had never learned to fear the surface.

On the command deck, a young Guard pressed his palm to the viewport, then pulled it back sharply, as if the cold had reached through the crystal. His hand was shaking. Lukas looked down at his own hand. It was shaking too.

The pressure wasn't loud. That was the terrible part of it. The ocean did not roar at them, did not rage; it simply applied — a patient, weighty mathematics of its own — and every rivet in the hull became a question the ship had to answer again, and each answer cost something.

"First time down," Kaelen said from behind Lukas, his voice stripped of its usual mockery. "The sea tells you the truth about your bones. It doesn't care how bright you burn on the surface. Down here, light is just another thing that can be crushed."

Lukas let the shiver pass through him and out. "Then we burn anyway."

Kaelen's jaw tightened — not quite a smile, but close. "The moon remembers those who endure," he said. "I used to think that was a war-slogan. Now I think it's a weather report."
