Alyra helped Lukas to a bench beside the command console, her hands finding a gash in his forearm that he hadn't noticed until she pressed a cloth to it. In the corner, Nyssa worked on Kaelen's ruined arm, her claws working through the opened actuator housing with a surgeon's precision, cataloguing every stressed seam.

"Six weeks of work, this arm," she said quietly, so only he could hear. "You used your plating as a shield. For a human."

"For the ship," Kaelen corrected. "And for the boy who held the ward node with his bare hands while his nose bled down his front. Lukas poured his into a cracked crystal and held the line."

"Of course he did." Nyssa's voice was flat. "That's the problem."

Kaelen studied her. "You don't trust him."

"I trust him more than he deserves. That's the trouble." Nyssa didn't look up from the servos. "He believes this ship will reach the Selkhar capital instead of being crushed into a speck of grey marble on the way. He believes the bond with his brother can be rebuilt. Faith is a beautiful weapon, Kaelen. It's also the reason every leader I've ever loved has died with their hand still extended."

"He held the ward," Kaelen said again, as if that settled it. "I felt his frequency in that water. It was Order — true Order, the kind the Spire forgot how to sing. Whatever else Lukas Wolfheart is, he is not a liar."

"No." Nyssa sealed the last housing with a soft click. "He's a dreamer. Which is worse. Liars know they're lying. Dreamers die believing the world is one good intention away from salvation."

Kaelen flexed the repaired arm, the servos whining back to life. "Then we stay alive long enough to prove the dreamer right. Or long enough to make sure he doesn't die alone."

Nyssa looked across the command center at Lukas, then at the prow beyond him, where Fenris stood motionless in his storm. Two brothers. One burning silver, one drowning in static, the same blood humming beneath both frequencies. The moon remembered both of them. Whether it remembered them as one thing or as two was the only question that mattered.

"The alliance holds," she said at last. "For now. Because the sea is crushing us all together. The real test comes when the crushing stops."

Kaelen made a sound that was almost a laugh. "Then let's hope the crushing doesn't stop."
