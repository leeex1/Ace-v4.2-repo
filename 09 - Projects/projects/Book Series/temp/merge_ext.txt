For three heartbeats they lived in both places at once, and it was the closest thing either had known to drowning. Lukas felt the tunnel-sweat on his brother's skin, the grinding pressure of the Oakhaven Shard, the precise weight of a hilt that was not his; Fenris felt the keel-shudder of the *Aurora* through boots he had never worn, the sting of windburn on feathers he did not have, the awful, bright love in a chest that was not his own. Two bodies, one mind, and the mind was tearing at the seams. When a Synth-Stalker lunged at Fenris in the roots, Lukas's shield-arm came up on the flight deck, and the silver dome over the fleet flickered grey; when a Chimera dove at the *Aurora*'s prow, Fenris's claws half-unsheathed in the dark, gouging furrows into the obsidian floor. They were each other's phantom, reaching across the miles for a sword that belonged to the other hand.

The Auditor watched them flail, and it was the closest thing to joy the Predator had ever allowed itself. Two variables, it seemed to whisper, already resolving into one. How efficient. How tidy. How nearly finished.

And that—more than the pain, more than the silver static weeping from Lukas's nose—was what broke them apart: the cold, arithmetic voice sliding between their minds, reminding them of what they were becoming. A chord was not a collapse. A chord was two notes that refused to become one.
