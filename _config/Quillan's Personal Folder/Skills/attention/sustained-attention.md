# Sustained Attention

## Overview
Sustained attention is the ability to maintain focused awareness over extended periods — essential for monitoring tasks, vigilance, and prolonged cognitive work. This sub-skill covers vigilance decrement, arousal theory, and strategies for maintaining attention over time.

## Core Concepts
- **Vigilance Decrement**: Performance on sustained attention tasks declines over time, typically within 15-30 minutes. Detection accuracy drops, response times slow, and false alarms increase.
- **Arousal and Performance (Yerkes-Dodson Law)**: Performance peaks at moderate arousal levels. Too little arousal leads to underperformance; too much leads to anxiety-driven impairment. Optimal arousal varies by task complexity.
- **Temporal Factors**: Attention fluctuates in cycles — ultradian rhythms (90-120 minute cycles) suggest optimal work/rest intervals. The first and last portions of a vigilance period typically show better performance (the vigilance function).
- **Countermeasures**: Task variation, frequent breaks, target salience enhancement, feedback on performance, and self-talk strategies reduce vigilance decrement. Stimulants (caffeine) provide temporary enhancement.

## Application
Design sustained attention tasks with frequent breaks, varied stimuli, and performance feedback. Schedule demanding vigilance tasks for peak alertness periods. Use the Pomodoro technique or similar interval methods.
