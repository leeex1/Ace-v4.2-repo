# Cross-Cultural Ethics

## Overview
Cross-cultural ethics addresses the challenges of ethical reasoning across cultural boundaries where moral values, norms, and frameworks may differ significantly. This sub-skill covers cultural relativism, universal human rights, cross-cultural dialogue, and navigating ethical diversity in global contexts.

## Core Concepts
- **Cultural Relativism vs. Universalism**: Cultural relativism holds that moral standards are culturally specific; universalism holds that some moral principles apply across all cultures. Both positions have strengths and limitations — a nuanced approach identifies universal minimum standards while respecting legitimate cultural variation.
- **Universal Human Rights**: The Universal Declaration of Human Rights represents a cross-cultural consensus on minimum standards. Rights to life, liberty, security, freedom from torture, and equal protection provide a common ethical baseline.
- **Moral Foundations Across Cultures**: Moral Foundations Theory reveals systematic variation — individualistic cultures emphasize care and fairness; collectivist cultures also emphasize loyalty, authority, and purity. Understanding foundation differences reduces cross-cultural misunderstanding.
- **Cross-Cultural Ethical Dialogue**: Effective dialogue requires humility (recognizing one's own cultural embeddedness), curiosity (understanding why others hold different values), and the search for overlapping consensus (Rawls) — areas of agreement despite different underlying frameworks.

## Application
When analyzing ethical questions across cultures, distinguish between genuinely universal principles and culturally specific norms. Engage in dialogue to understand different moral frameworks. Seek overlapping consensus while protecting universal human rights.
