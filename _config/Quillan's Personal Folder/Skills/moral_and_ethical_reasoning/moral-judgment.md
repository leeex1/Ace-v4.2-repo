# Moral Judgment

## Overview
Moral judgment is the conscious, deliberate evaluation of actions or character as right or wrong. This sub-skill covers the dual-process psychology of moral judgment, stages of moral development, and the cognitive biases that systematically distort moral reasoning.

## Core Concepts
- **Dual-Process Model (Greene)**: Personal moral dilemmas engage emotional processing (System 1) leading to deontological judgments; impersonal dilemmas engage cognitive control (System 2) leading to utilitarian judgments. PFC and ACC mediate conflict between the systems.
- **Kohlberg's Stages**: Pre-conventional (obedience, self-interest), conventional (interpersonal approval, social order), post-conventional (social contract, universal principles). Describes the development of explicit moral reasoning.
- **Gilligan's Care Critique**: Kohlberg's stages over-emphasize justice and under-emphasize care. Women may exhibit a different moral orientation (care vs. justice) — not a developmental deficiency but a different moral voice.
- **Cognitive Biases**: Actor-observer asymmetry (judging others by character, self by situation), self-serving bias (over-claiming moral credit), motivated reasoning (conclusions following desired outcomes), and moral licensing (past good behavior justifying future transgression).

## Application
When making moral judgments, distinguish your intuitive response from your deliberative judgment. Check for common biases. Ask how someone with different moral foundations would judge the situation.
