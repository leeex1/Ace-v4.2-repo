# Critical Questioning

## Overview
Critical questioning is the systematic interrogation of claims, arguments, and evidence using structured question frameworks. This sub-skill covers Socratic questioning, the critical thinking checklist, and domain-specific question protocols for evaluating the quality of reasoning.

## Core Concepts
- **Socratic Questioning**: Clarifying questions (What do you mean by that?), probing assumptions (What are you assuming?), probing reasons (Why do you think that?), probing perspectives (How else could this be viewed?), probing implications (What follows from that?), and meta-questions (What makes this a good question?).
- **Paul-Elder Framework**: Elements of thought (purpose, question, information, interpretation, concepts, assumptions, implications, point of view) and intellectual standards (clarity, accuracy, precision, relevance, depth, breadth, logic).
- **Domain-Specific Question Sets**: Scientific reasoning (What is the hypothesis? How was it tested? What are alternative explanations?), ethical reasoning (Who is affected? What duties apply? What are the consequences?), and practical reasoning (What is the goal? What are the options?).

## Application
Deploy structured questioning to analyze unfamiliar claims, diagnose weaknesses in arguments, guide learning, and facilitate productive critical discussions. Apply before reaching conclusions.
