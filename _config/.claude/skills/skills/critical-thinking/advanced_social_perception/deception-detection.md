# Deception Detection

## Overview
Deception detection identifies when someone is intentionally misleading others through lies, omissions, or distortions. While no single cue reliably indicates deception, multi-channel analysis can identify inconsistencies that suggest deceptive intent. This sub-skill covers verbal, non-verbal, and contextual indicators of deception.

## Core Concepts
- **Verbal Indicators**: Linguistic evasion, over-specificity, contradictions, and plausibility issues
- **Non-Verbal Leakage**: Micro-expressions, vocal stress, and incongruent body language
- **Cognitive Load Cues**: Increased hesitation, reduced detail, and rigid posture under questioning
- **Baseline Comparison**: Comparing current behavior to established patterns for anomaly detection
- **Contextual Plausibility**: Assessing whether claims are consistent with known facts and incentives

## Application
No single behavior is diagnostic of deception. The key is detecting inconsistencies between channels (e.g., verbal content contradicts emotional expression). Establish behavioral baselines before making judgments. Consider that honest individuals under stress may exhibit cues that resemble deception.
