# Abductive Reasoning

## Overview
Abductive reasoning — inference to the best explanation — moves from an observation to the most likely cause or explanation. It is the primary mode of reasoning used in diagnosis, scientific hypothesis generation, and everyday explanatory inference.

## Core Concepts
- **Inference to the Best Explanation (IBE)**: Given evidence E and candidate explanations H1...Hn, select the hypothesis that best explains E. Evaluation criteria include explanatory power, parsimony (Occam's razor), coherence with background knowledge, and testability.
- **Diagnostic Reasoning**: Working from symptoms to root cause. Differential diagnosis methodology enumerates possible causes and systematically eliminates them through testing.
- **Hypothesis Generation**: Creating plausible explanatory hypotheses from observed phenomena. Abduction is the creative engine of scientific discovery — generating candidates that deduction and induction then test.
- **Abductive Fallacies**: Confirmation bias (seeking only confirming evidence), ad hoc hypothesizing (unfalsifiable additions), circular reasoning, and the argument from ignorance.

## Application
Apply abduction in debugging (symptoms → root cause), medical diagnosis, forensic analysis, and scientific theory formation. Always generate multiple candidate explanations and design tests to discriminate between them.
