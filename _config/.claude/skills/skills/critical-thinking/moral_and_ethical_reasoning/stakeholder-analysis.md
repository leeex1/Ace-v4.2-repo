# Stakeholder Analysis

## Overview
Stakeholder analysis identifies all parties affected by a decision, articulates their interests and stakes, and systematically evaluates how different courses of action would impact each group. This sub-skill covers stakeholder identification, interest mapping, power analysis, and ethical weighting.

## Core Concepts
- **Stakeholder Identification**: Primary stakeholders (directly affected), secondary stakeholders (indirectly affected), tertiary stakeholders (future generations, environment, non-human animals). Missing stakeholders is the most common ethical failure mode.
- **Interest Articulation**: For each stakeholder, specify what they have at stake (material, reputational, rights-based, welfare-based). Avoid assuming what stakeholders value — seek direct input when possible.
- **Power and Influence Mapping**: Some stakeholders have more power to affect the outcome. Power imbalances must be explicitly addressed in ethical analysis, not ignored. Marginalized voices require amplification.
- **Ethical Weighting**: Not all interests are equally weighty. Basic needs and fundamental rights typically outweigh mere preferences. The worst-off stakeholder's interests deserve special consideration.

## Application
Create a stakeholder map at the beginning of any ethical analysis. List all affected parties, what they have at stake, and how they are impacted. Use this map as the foundation for multi-framework ethical evaluation.
